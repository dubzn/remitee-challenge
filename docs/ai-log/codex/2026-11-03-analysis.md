## Independent assessment

The contract's business state machine is small. The main risks are boundary correctness, nonstandard token behavior, callbacks during creation/payout, and consistent event recovery. The backend must not report refund completion solely because time elapsed.

The strongest scope is one non-upgradeable escrow contract plus one Node.js service with an indexer, API, and SQLite. Avoid introducing production treasury/custody requirements from the JD. The scoring gives 70% to contract security, logic, and tests, making those the first delivery priority.

Use a durable scanner for live observation and recovery. Following the independent review, the proposal uses one polling worker and atomic bounded-range commits rather than a watcher plus fallback and per-block commits. Preserve event identity/order and halt honestly on a canonical mismatch. A small projection can be explicitly rebuilt after validating deployment identity rather than supporting arbitrary fork rollback.

Time-derived status needs two concepts: recorded chain outcome and current eligibility. A TTL crossing does not move funds. Chain time is especially important because the required demo advances the local node's time.

No owner or proxy is needed. SafeERC20 covers return-value incompatibility; it does not solve fees, rebases, blacklists, or malicious balances. The invariant suggested by the challenge needs a donation caveat: direct transfers can make actual balance exceed pending escrow liabilities.

One additional validation worth settling is beneficiary equal to the escrow contract, which can strand a released amount. This is an explicit proposed assumption, not an original requirement.
